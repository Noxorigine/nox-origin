STRONG_EVIDENCE:
High-resolution, paired micro-stratigraphic datasets (sub-millimeter µ-XRF trace element profiles combined with continuous δ18O/δ13C micro-sampling) showing reproducible, distinct geochemical signatures: sharp, localized detrital/ash peaks (Ti/Ca, K/Ca) alongside truncated isotopic cycles without evaporative enrichment indicating abrupt mechanical descaling, versus gradual temperature-correlated Mg/Ca progressions and covariant δ18O/δ13C enrichment preceding growth cessation indicating climate-driven drought.

WEAK_EVIDENCE:

CONTRADICTION_SIGNAL:
Studies demonstrating that detrital spikes (Ti/Ca) and abrupt isotopic truncations can occur naturally during seasonal flash-floods or sudden karst hydrogeological shifts without human intervention, or sources arguing that evaporative and kinetic fractionation signatures during drought events mimic the geochemical signal of post-cleaning flow resumption.
